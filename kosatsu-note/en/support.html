Guide & support

Theory Notes is a notebook for the things you notice in stories and themes. It runs on iPhone with iOS 26 or later, iPad with iPadOS 26 or later, and Mac with macOS 26 or later. Notes are saved on each device. To continue writing on another device, turn on iCloud Sync on each one.

For version 1.18.0 · Updated November 25, 2026. Earlier versions may look different. English support requires version 1.11.0 or later.

Language and guided practice

Theory Notes uses the app language selected on your device and supports English and Japanese. On iPhone or iPad, choose the language in the app’s entry in Settings. On Mac, open System Settings → General → Language & Region and use the Applications list.

The interface, guide, practice notebook and AI guidance use the selected language. The support page and privacy policy you open from Settings use that language too. Changing it does not translate or change your saved work names, note text, people, tags or connections.

When you first open an empty Library, choose Try the sample → Try It to practice from the beginning. You’ll start by adding a TV series. The practice notebook is separate from your own.

To practice a particular task, open Settings → Try the App. Under Choose a chapter, you can start with any of the seven chapters. They cover Add a Series, Episodes and Notes, Resolve Questions, Link notes, Write a connected note, Record Payoffs, and Review.

Write and review notes

  1. Choose Add work and select the type of work, such as a TV series, movie, book or game. Seasons, episodes and official links are optional.
  2. Choose Write a note in the Library. When you open a work, Note destination or Last note destination at the top shows where you’ll be writing. In the editor, tap the destination to change the episode or write about the work as a whole. For a series or anime with no previous destination, the app selects the first episode in season order, skipping empty seasons. Movie notes belong to the main feature.
  3. Starting in 1.16.1, open a work with registered episodes and tap Choose where to write at the top. Pick another episode or Overall to start writing. If a draft exists for that destination, it opens so you can continue. If the last episode is unavailable, for example because it’s in the Trash, tap Choose a new destination to pick an available one.
  4. Your writing saves to a draft as you type. Choose Save and continue to save a new note and start another, or Done to finish a new or existing note and return. You can resume unfinished writing from drafts.
  5. Review notes in the Notes tab with search and filters. Opening a work or episode's note list applies its context. Show all clears the search and filters.

In a series or anime, when the destination at the top is an episode, Overall notes lets you write or read notes about the work as a whole. If an episode has no notes yet, open its Notes list and choose Write the first note for this episode.

Each episode row, including the last episode you selected, shows Write a note or Resume draft along with its note list. In the editor, choose characters or tags and close the picker to return to your writing.

To edit a saved note, tap the pencil at the top right on iPhone or iPad, or click Edit above the note on Mac. Use the connections section to link another note, or Add additional note to add a supplement. Sharing, pinning and moving a note to the trash are in the Note actions menu.

Starting in version 1.17.1, when the list and detail appear on separate screens on iPhone or iPad, swipe right from the left edge of a note or work to reveal its list as you drag. Your search and filters stay in place when you return. Tap a row in the list to open its note.

Starting in version 1.17.1, tap the active tab again on iPhone or iPad to scroll to the top of the current screen. You can do this while keeping a work’s detail or Release Notes open.

Cards and shortcuts on iPhone and iPad

Version 1.18.0 shows works and notes as cards. To switch back to the previous list style, choose the list layout in Settings. Touch and hold a note to preview its text. Tap the checklist icon to select multiple notes and add tags or pin them together.

In Settings, open Note Swipe Actions to choose which actions appear for each direction and in what order. Swipe partway to choose an action or all the way to run the first one. You can also turn interaction haptics on or off on supported devices.

Optional details and organization

A title is optional. Settings → Note Input Settings controls which fields appear in the editor and which are grouped under details. People, tags, note type, reference photos, links and a playback position or page number can be added when needed. Changing these display settings keeps saved information.

Use page numbers for novels and manga. Other notes can use a playback position where appropriate. Pinning and official links are in the details. You can also refer to other notes and return to your writing.

The season and episode management screen lets you add, rename and reorder seasons or episodes. Returning the planned episode count to an unspecified value does not delete existing episodes or notes. Edit a person's name and notes together from the work's people and tags screen. While selecting people for a note, use the information button to open their notes, then choose Edit person. Renaming a person keeps their note associations.

Write and search on Mac

Select a work in the sidebar to see its notes in the middle column. On the work’s page, click Notes under Last note destination to see the notes saved there.

Click a note in the middle column to read it on the right. Double-click the row to edit the note. You can also click the title or body on the right to start editing that field. Click Save or press ⌘S to save your changes.

With a work or episode selected, press ⌘N to write a note there. While browsing or reading, press ⌘F and type to search the notes in the middle column. To search across works, first choose All notes at the top of the work list.

To write another note in the same place, choose Save and continue in the editor. In the tag picker, type a new tag name and press Return to add it. Press ⌘Return when you have finished choosing tags.

Use Text size at the bottom of the sidebar to choose Standard, Large, or Extra large. Work headings, episode counts, action buttons, and photo captions also use the selected size. When setting a playback position, choose the hours, minutes, and seconds from the menus, or select Type directly and enter minutes and seconds, such as 01:23.

iPad and landscape

On a wide iPad screen, the work or note list appears beside the detail. Use the button at the top left of the list to hide it, then the button at the top left of the detail to show it again. Search text, filters and the selected note are retained. Reference notes open beside your writing when there is enough space. iPhone also supports portrait and both landscape orientations.

In settings and the note list, tap a switch's label or surrounding row space as well as the switch itself to change its setting.

You can tap anywhere in a work, note or saved-search row, including its edges.

Cover images and official pages

Retrieve an official URL's shared image or select a visible area of the page as the work's image. Images are stored on the device. Official sites and character charts open in the standard in-app Safari browser.

Search

In Library, search work titles, filter by type and progress, and sort manually, by recently opened, or by title. Manual reordering is available with no filters in manual order.

In Notes, open the bookmark button “Saved searches” to name and save the current query and filters, apply a saved search, rename it, or delete it. These preferences stay on this device and are not included in notebook backup or sync. Unavailable destinations are retained until you adjust or reset the filter. Matching note text is highlighted with a surrounding excerpt.

Use the search field above the list on the Notes tab to search the entire saved note body, titles, people and tags. Work, episode and type filters, as well as the option to hide resolved notes, also affect results. Use Show all to clear the search and filters.

In an episode’s note list, tap Pinned or Open clues and questions to narrow the results while keeping your search text and other filters. Tap the same filter again to turn it off. Payoffs and answers in this episode opens the pairs of notes for the whole episode, showing each original clue or question alongside its payoff or answer.

Link notes searches entire note bodies among saved notes in the same work. Narrow the results by episode or choose work-wide notes only. Use Clear search and episode filters if you cannot find a note. A connected result shows its relationship and a pencil icon. Tap it to edit the connection.

Review So Far

For a series or anime, open Review → Review So Far. The view opens through the last episode marked as watched. If none are marked as watched, choose the last episode to include from the numbered list. Use Review Through at the top to change the range.

The selector uses the current season and episode list order and keeps future episode titles hidden. The view shows unanswered questions, unresolved clues and pinned notes through the selected episode, with connections only inside that range. Answers from later episodes do not mark earlier questions as resolved in this view.

Expand a note row to read its body and view its photos. This is a read-only view. Work-wide notes and supplements are excluded because their story position is unknown. Text and photos reflect their currently saved content; this does not restore a note to a past edit or detect spoilers typed into an earlier note.

Connections and the map

  1. Open Link notes in a note's connections section.
  2. Select one or more notes and compare their text. Notes can belong to different episodes of the same work.
  3. For each note, choose a relationship such as Related, Evidence, Counterevidence or Payoff / Resolution, set its direction and save. You can add a comment. Connections remain when you change a note’s type.

Each pair of notes can have one connection. Open Edit connection from a linked note or a line on the map. Choose the relationship between the two note cards, use the up-and-down arrows to reverse the direction, or choose Change linked note to select a different note.

To merge older connections between the same two notes, open the connection you want to keep and choose Keep This Connection. Confirm the merge to retain the other connections’ comments in the remaining connection.

Choose Write a new note and link it to open the editor with the other note already selected. Saving the new note also saves the connection. A draft keeps the other note, relationship, direction and comment if you stop writing. If you open an existing connection from the list to edit it, the notes you selected and the comment you entered are still there when you go back. If you change the other end of the original connection before finishing a draft, saving the draft keeps that change and links the new note to the note selected in the draft. Questions linked as Related stay open until you record an answer.

For a question, choose Record Resolution. For a clue, choose Record Payoff. Select an existing answer or choose Write a new note. To finish it later, choose Close as Draft. Open the original note and tap Resume draft answer or Resume draft payoff to pick up your text and connection comment. Saving the answer marks the question as resolved or the clue as paid off. On the answer note, the connection reads Resolved question, Clue paid off or Resolved note, depending on the original note’s type.

On the connection map, drag to move and pinch or use the zoom controls to change scale. Overview fits the visible notes. Center returns to the starting note at the default scale. If you opened the map without a starting note, use Reset. On Mac, click a card to highlight its connections or double-click to open the note. On iPhone and iPad, double-tap to open it. To create a connection, choose Link, select the source card, then search for the other note.

Tap a line to review or edit its relationship, direction and comment. To see one card’s direct connections, select it and open View Connections. The list opens on Selected Note. Switch to All on Map to see the other connections on the current map, even if the selected note has none.

On iPhone and iPad, touch and hold a card, then drag it onto another. On Mac, drag one card onto another with the mouse or trackpad. This opens an existing connection or lets you choose a new relationship and save it.

AI suggestions

AI requires a device that supports Apple Intelligence, with the feature enabled and the model ready. Comparisons run on the device. Writing, search and manual linking work without AI.

  1. From a work’s Review section, choose Find AI suggestions in this work. Choose a comparison scope and tap Find AI suggestions. You can also use Find AI suggestions for this note in the linking screen.
  2. Work-wide suggestions show pairs of unconnected notes. The review screen places the notes and relationship first, followed by the reason and both notes in full.
  3. Confirm or change the relationship and direction, and save only what you choose. Showing a suggestion does not create a connection.

In Settings → Note Input Settings, Automatically Search for AI Connections controls whether the app looks for suggestions as you write or save a new note. With the Simple input style and automatic search off, you can start by typing the note body.

To run a search yourself, enter the note text and open Details → Find AI suggestions for this note. This runs one search with the current text, both for a new note and while editing a saved note. Progress and suggestions appear at the top of the editor.

AI compares beginning excerpts of long notes. This differs from full-text search and may miss connections. If AI is unavailable, fails or finds nothing, search and link notes manually.

Import text

In Library, tap Import notes (the down-arrow button at the top right), then Paste and Organize Text to paste plain text. If you shared text to Theory Notes from another app, open it under Text Waiting to Be Imported. Text shared in UTF-16 format is supported. To read text in a photo you’ve attached, see Reference photos.

  1. Choose the destination work. If you choose Add work and save a new work, it becomes the selected destination. When another work is already selected, Add work is under Location Details. You can also change the episode or note type there.
  2. For a single note, choose Import as One Note. To divide the text, use Make Selection a Note in the text menu or Split Here at the cursor. On supported devices, AI can also suggest how to split the text.
  3. Check each candidate's body, type and destination. Edit, split or merge candidates as needed, then import. If the episode number in the original text differs from the AI suggestion, choose the destination under Review and Edit. If you choose Undo Last Extraction immediately after taking out a passage, emoji and line breaks return to their original positions.

If you import only part of the text, you can resume the remainder from Text Waiting to Be Imported. To discard an item, swipe left on its row or touch and hold it, then choose Remove from Pending Imports and confirm. This removes only the pending text in that row. Notes you already imported and the original text in the other app stay in place.

Reference photos

Each note can hold up to 10 photos. On Mac, open the note on the right and drag images from Finder or Photos into the Drop images here area. You can drag several at once or click the area to choose files. While reading, the images are attached to the open note. While writing, they are added to the note you’re editing. On iPhone or iPad, use the photo button in the editor or details to pick, take or paste a photo. Open an attachment and choose “Recognize Text in Photo”. Recognition runs on this device and does not require Apple Intelligence. Select the lines to include, correct recognition errors, then choose “Append to Body” while writing or “Add as a Supplement” for a saved note. Canceling leaves the note unchanged. The original photo and existing text are kept; recognized text may contain errors.

Photos keep their original proportions. Uniform black bars may be hidden for display only. Tap to enlarge; where bars are hidden, switch to the original photo to see the full image. This does not change the saved image or backups.

Copy places the saved image on this device's clipboard. The app does not save it to Photos or send it directly through the share sheet. Removing an attachment keeps the note body and the original in Photos. Reference photos are included in complete backups and enabled iCloud sync, but not text exports.

Please respect the rights and usage terms of any images you use or copy.

Export and share

To share one note, open its Note actions menu and choose Export or share text. To share several, use the work’s menu or open Note display and sharing in the Notes tab with a work or episode selected. An export for a work or episode includes all its saved notes, even if a search or other filters narrow the list you’re viewing.

Choose Text Only to share just the note body. The first time you tap Choose Fields, Included Fields opens with only the body selected. Turn on any other details you want, such as titles or characters. Your selection is remembered for next time. Check the preview, then share the text or save a plain text (.txt) or Markdown (.md) file. If you include connections in a Markdown export, you can jump to related notes in the same file, even when their headings contain square brackets or backslashes.

Only the saved text shown in the preview is exported. Photos, drafts and trash are excluded. A text export is not a complete restorable backup.

Sync, backup and restore

iCloud sync and automatic backup are separate features. Both are off by default. Your notebook is stored locally. Enable each feature explicitly in Settings if you want to use it.

Sync between devices

Sign in to the same Apple Account on each iPhone, iPad or Mac you want to use. In Theory Notes on each device, open Settings → iCloud Sync and turn it on. On Mac, Settings is at the bottom of the sidebar. Sync includes notes, reference photos, drafts and trash. Incoming changes wait until you close the editor. Turning sync off keeps the data already saved.

If sync reports a conflict, look under Differences by work on the same screen. Each work shows how many records exist only on this device, only in iCloud, or on both with different content. Expand a work to compare the changed passages, character names, tags or playback times. For supplements, you can compare the text and its source. A timestamp difference alone does not cause a conflict. Photo changes show the photo count on each side.

Choose Use All Data from This Device or Use All Data from iCloud and confirm. The entire notebook will use the selected side’s data, including records without a conflict. If the data changes during review and a conflict remains, compare the updated differences and choose again. Before applying the choice, the app keeps a copy of each side on this device. You can export either copy as JSON under Recovery Data from the Last Conflict.

Automatic backup

Open Settings → Backup → iCloud. Enabling automatic backup stores complete snapshots, including works, episodes, notes, connections, classifications, cover images, reference photos, drafts and trash, in your private app-specific iCloud storage. It requires iCloud Drive, a network connection and sufficient iCloud space.

A new generation is created only when content changes. An unreadable backup in the list does not cause the app to keep saving copies of the same content. The app keeps this installation's latest five generations and the last generation for each of the latest seven days with a backup. Older generations are cleaned up after a new upload is confirmed. Legacy-format backups and backups from other installations are not automatically cleaned up.

Check the status and last confirmed upload time. Pending, uploading or failed does not mean the latest content is safely in iCloud. Confirm completion before removing the app. Backup snapshots do not merge edits between devices.

Restore after reinstalling

  1. On the iPhone, iPad or Mac you are restoring to, sign in with the same Apple Account and enable iCloud Drive.
  2. If your Library is empty, choose Restore from iCloud backup, available even on the first screen. Restorable Backups opens with the list expanded. With an existing library, open Settings → Backup → iCloud, then Restorable Backups.
  3. Check the date and record counts, then tap Review Backup Contents for the backup you want. Choose Replace All Data and confirm. Restoring replaces the entire current notebook. You can export the previous notebook from Settings → Backup → Local.

Only a completed backup can be used after reinstalling. Local-only data and pending uploads are not guaranteed to be recoverable. Empty initial data does not automatically overwrite an existing iCloud backup.

Stop or delete automatic backups

Turning automatic backup off stops future automatic uploads and keeps existing backups. To remove one, select it in the app and confirm the action to stop automatic backup and delete it. Notes on the device and other backups remain.

Local backups

Use Settings → Backup → Local to save a complete JSON backup, including cover images, reference photos, drafts and trash. Select a local backup file to review and restore it. Restoring replaces all current data.

Version 1.17.0 can restore backup formats v1, v2, v3, v5, v6, v7 and v8. The format depends on what you save. For example, page numbers use v7 or later, and a draft with a preselected connection uses v8. To retrieve the notebook that was on your device just before the last restore, choose Export Backup from Before Last Restore on the Local screen. If a later restore fails, you can still export the copy from before the last successful restore.

Troubleshooting

A saved note is missing from the list

Check the selected work and episode, filters, search text and trash. Use Show all to clear the search and filters. If you stopped writing before saving the note, check the work’s drafts. If a save error appeared, check its message.

A note appears in search but not when linking

Check that it is a saved note in the same work and clear episode filters. The source itself, drafts, additional details and trash are excluded. Existing connections show their status and reason. See Search.

AI suggestions are unavailable

Check device compatibility, Apple Intelligence settings and model readiness. You can always search and link notes manually if AI is unavailable, fails or finds no candidates.

I moved a note to the trash by mistake

Restore it from Settings → Trash. Permanently deleted content requires a backup made before deletion. Restoring a backup replaces the whole notebook.

iCloud backup is not progressing

Check automatic backup settings, your Apple Account, iCloud Drive, network and available iCloud space. Review the in-app status and try backing up now. Do not remove the app while the latest upload is pending.

A cover image or official page does not load

Check the URL and network. A page may lack a shared image, have been removed or restrict access. You can select a visible page area from the work's editor to set a cover image.

Do I need an internet connection?

Local writing, organization and search work offline. Official pages, cover retrieval, iCloud sync, and iCloud backup and restore require a connection.

I cannot share a reference photo

Direct photo sharing and saving to Photos are not provided, and text exports exclude photos. Use Copy in the photo viewer to paste on this device, subject to the image's usage terms.
